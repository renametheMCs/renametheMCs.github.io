Additional reading

As scientists, we fully believe that everyone should have the chance to do their own research on this issue and make up their own minds. Here are some suggestions for places to start; please contact us if there are other sources you think we should add.

Primary historical sources

  • Pigafetta, A. Magellan's Voyage Around the World. (link)
      • Antonio Pigafetta was a scribe who joined Magellan's expedition and produced the primary first-hand account of Magellan's journey. The free version linked above shows the original Italian side by side with an English translation.

Other related sources

  • Dennefeld, Michel. "A History of the Magellanic Clouds and the European Exploration of the Southern Hemisphere." The Messenger: 181, 37-42. (2020). (link)
      • An overview by an astronomer of how the MCs got their names, with several references to primary sources.
  • Bergreen, L. Over the Edge of the World: Magellan's Terrifying Circumnavigation of the Globe. HarperCollins, 2019.
      • This book is somewhat sensational, but does discuss the historical context of Magellan's voyage in an accessible way, and the bibliography contains citations to several primary and secondary sources.

Press about the movement to rename the Clouds

  • Johnston, H. "The Magellanic Clouds: astronomers make the case for a name change (podcast)." Physics World, 2024 Jan 18. (link)
  • Ravisetti, M. "The Magellanic Clouds must be renamed, astronomers say." Space.com, 2023 Nov 3. (link)
  • Carpineti, A. "It's Time The Magellanic Clouds Are Renamed, Astronomers Say." IFLScience, 2023 Oct 21. (link)
  • de los Reyes, M. "Astronomers Need to Rename the Magellanic Clouds." Physics (APS opinion articles): 16, 152. (2023) (link)
  • Conover, E. "Astronomers call for renaming the Magellanic Clouds." Science News, 2023 Sept 20. (link)
